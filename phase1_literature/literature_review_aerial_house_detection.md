# Literature Review — Aerial House Detection (bounding boxes + confidence + counting)

**Scope:** 2021–2026 papers on aerial image object detection, building/house detection from satellite & aerial imagery, rooftop detection, small-object detection in remote sensing, UAV video detection, and domain adaptation. Fundamental older papers (Faster R-CNN, DETR, ClusDet, DOTA) included where essential.

**Verification method:** every paper below was confirmed via the Semantic Scholar API (metadata, venue, year, citation counts) and/or the arXiv API (title, authors, abstract) and GitHub API (repositories). Metrics are quoted from the verified abstracts/papers; anything I could not confirm from the source is explicitly marked **not verified**.

## 2. ANALYSIS

### 2.1 Which approaches are most relevant for a house-detection project?

**ASSESSMENT (my judgment, grounded in the verified facts above):**

1. **A modern YOLO-family detector (YOLOv8/v11 or YOLO-NAS) trained on a house/building dataset, plus SAHI slicing inference** is the highest-evidence combination. Three independent lines support it:
   - YOLO-style detectors are the base of the best-performing recent aerial models (YOLO-MARS: 40.9 mAP50 on VisDrone2019, verified; SR-YOLO: +6.3 mAP50 over YOLOv8s, verified; BBD: YOLO-NAS F1 0.975 for buildings, verified).
   - SAHI adds +5–7 AP on aerial small objects *without retraining* (verified) and is detector-agnostic, so it composes with any of the above.
   - For a small team, YOLO gives the shortest training-loop (Ultralytics single-command training), unlike Oriented-DETR/RT-DETR-class models which need heavier tooling.

2. **Two-stage/cluster-based methods (ClusDet, YOLC, QueryDet)** matter when the scene is *sparsely distributed* houses over a huge frame — they zoom compute into dense clusters instead of tiling everything. For house detection specifically, buildings usually cover a large fraction of the frame, so naive SAHI tiling is often competitive; cluster-based zooming wins when houses are a minority (rural scenes, roads/fields dominating).

3. **Segmentation (U-Net/WHU/Inria line) vs detection:** the majority of building-extraction literature is *semantic segmentation* (WHU, Inria), not box detection. Segmentation is better for footprint area/boundaries; box detection is what the project needs (counting, confidence, faster annotation). The practical hybrid from the literature is BBD's recipe (verified): detect boxes with YOLO-NAS, refine boundaries with SAM (IoU 0.912). If the project needs counting only, boxes suffice; skip SAM.

4. **Oriented bounding boxes (OBB, DOTA line):** only worth the complexity if houses are significantly rotated relative to the grid (gabled roofs at odd angles with narrow lots). For counting-oriented tasks in grid-layout settlements (most housing estates), horizontal boxes with rotated augmentation are usually enough. **ASSESSMENT**, not a verified fact.

5. **Domain adaptation:** the Ankara SkySat study (verified) shows a US-trained Mask R-CNN reached F1 0.92–0.96 on new sensor+geography with only 5–53 local labels. For a small team, **few-shot fine-tuning beats full DA training pipelines** — the 2025 TGRS DA papers (FSDA-DETR, VLSDA) exist but are heavy machinery for marginal gains at this scale.

### 2.2 Open challenges (per the six asked areas)

**(a) Densely adjacent/attached houses.**
- FACT: ClusDet (2019) and YOLC (2024) both exist *because* non-uniform, dense object layouts break uniform detection grids; YOLC's Local Scale Module explicitly re-zooms into clusters.
- FACT: BBD (2024) found that tiling "divides buildings into too many parts" — buildings straddling tile borders lose F1, requiring fine-tuning to recover (0.744→0.883 for YOLOv5, 0.869→0.975 for YOLO-NAS).
- ASSESSMENT: row-house/terrace and connected-roof configurations are still poorly handled: NMS suppresses adjacent detections, and merged roofs produce one box for multiple dwellings. There is no strong benchmark isolating attached-house detection — this is a genuine open gap and a publishable angle.

**(b) Small objects in high-resolution imagery.**
- FACT: SAHI (2022) is the standard mitigation (+5–7 AP, detector-agnostic); AI-TOD (2021) formalizes the <20 px regime; QueryDet (2022) trades tiling cost for sparse queries.
- FACT: YOLO-MARS (2025) reaches only 23.4 mAP50:95 on VisDrone even with dedicated small-object modules — tiny-object *localization* (not just detection) remains far from solved.
- ASSESSMENT: the gap between mAP50 (40.9) and mAP50:95 (23.4) shows coarse detection is easy, precise localization of tiny structures is not. For houses this matters less than for vehicles (houses are larger), but drone-imagery houses at 5–10 cm GSD can still be sub-30 px.

**(c) Variation in roof shape/size/color.**
- FACT: DOTA 2.0 (TPAMI 2021) is built explicitly around "massive variations in the scale and orientation of objects caused by the bird's-eye view".
- FACT: the WUI study (Huang & Jin 2022) shows F1 drops from 0.75 (rural) to 0.62 (interface WUI) — heterogeneous, non-urban roof types degrade detection measurably even within one country.
- ASSESSMENT: color/material variation (metal vs tile vs thatch) interacts with sun angle and shadows; no public benchmark covers this diversity for houses. Strong data augmentation and multi-region training data is the only proven mitigation; no architectural fix dominates.

**(d) Occlusion by shadows/trees.**
- FACT: VisDrone's benchmark design (TPAMI 2021) includes occlusion as a core challenge dimension; UAV small-object papers (YOLO-MARS, SR-YOLO) report mutual occlusion as a leading failure cause.
- ASSESSMENT: for houses specifically, tree-canopy occlusion is *worse* than in vehicle detection because occlusion persists across acquisitions and seasons, and shadows from tall structures can hide entire roof planes. Verified literature is thin here — most mitigation is in shadow-aware segmentation papers (e.g., shadow-detection works in ISPRS venues), not box detection. This is an open problem; multi-temporal data fusion is the practical workaround but is under-studied in the object-detection frame.

**(e) Cross-region/cross-sensor domain shift.**
- FACT: Sawa et al. (2024, verified) quantify it positively — F1 0.92–0.96 transferring US→Türkiye with few-shot labels.
- FACT: dedicated DA methods exist (FSDA-DETR, VLSDA, FIE-Net — all IEEE TGRS 2025, verified), indicating the community treats DA as an active unsolved subfield.
- ASSESSMENT: satellite→drone shift is the harder direction (GSD 0.3–10 m vs 0.02–0.1 m; nadir vs oblique). The literature mostly studies satellite→satellite or aerial→aerial; **satellite-trained → drone-inferred house detection is under-benchmarked**, another publishable gap. Few-shot fine-tuning with ~50–100 local labels is the evidence-backed pragmatic route.

**(f) Counting accuracy.**
- FACT: counting is almost never the primary metric in the verified papers — they optimize mAP/F1, not count error (MAE/RMSE). The building-counting literature that exists is mostly segmentation-then-watershed.
- ASSESSMENT: mAP is a poor proxy for counting accuracy: a detector can have high mAP while systematically missing one house type (systematic count bias), and duplicate/missed detections in dense rows hurt counting more than average AP suggests. Practical implication for the project: report **count MAE and percentage error per tile** in addition to mAP — this is cheap to compute and differentiates the project from most prior work. No verified paper reviewed here directly optimizes counting error for houses; treat this as an open challenge.

### 2.3 Verdict — most practical modeling approach for a small research team

**ASSESSMENT:**

1. **Base detector:** YOLOv8/v11 (Ultralytics) — smallest training/eval loop, best-documented, transfer learning from COCO works out of the box; YOLO-NAS if boundary quality matters more.
2. **Inference:** SAHI slicing for full-frame aerial images (verified +5–7 AP without retraining); tune slice size to your house GSD, and be aware of the straddling-building problem BBD documents (fix: overlapping slices, which SAHI supports natively).
3. **Data:** start from WHU Building Dataset / Inria for pretraining intuition (segmentation), annotate 200–500 local boxes for the target region, then few-shot fine-tune (evidence from Sawa 2024 that this suffices).
4. **Skip for now:** oriented boxes (OBB), DETR-family training, full domain-adversarial DA pipelines, and SAM boundary refinement — all add complexity for marginal counting gains. Revisit OBB only if the study region has strongly rotated, tightly packed roofs.
5. **Differentiate the project** where the literature is thin: (i) attached/dense row-house counting, (ii) cross-sensor (satellite→drone) transfer with few labels, (iii) explicit counting-error (MAE) reporting. These are the open gaps identified above and are achievable with the stack in points 1–3.
